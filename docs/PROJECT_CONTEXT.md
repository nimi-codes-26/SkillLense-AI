# Project Context

## What SkillLens AI Is

SkillLens AI is a student skill-profiling and career-gap-analysis web application. A student
enters values for 9 skill/academic features (see [DATA_MODEL.md](DATA_MODEL.md)), and the
application returns:

1. A predicted **skill category** (a machine-learning classification into one of four
   categories discovered from data — see [ML_METHODOLOGY.md](ML_METHODOLOGY.md)).
2. A **career alignment score** against 5 fixed, illustrative career roles, including a
   best-match role and a per-role skill-gap breakdown (a rule-based calculation, not a
   machine-learning prediction — see [ML_METHODOLOGY.md](ML_METHODOLOGY.md)).

The system consists of three parts, developed and documented as three areas of the same
repository:

- `ml/` — data generation, exploratory analysis, clustering, classification, and model
  artifacts.
- `backend/` — a FastAPI service that loads the trained model artifacts and exposes them,
  plus the rule-based career alignment engine, over HTTP.
- `frontend/` — a React single-page application that collects a student's profile and
  displays the results.

## Problem It Addresses

Students often have limited visibility into how their current skills compare to what
different career paths typically require, and where the biggest gaps are. SkillLens AI gives
a structured, numeric view of a self-reported skill profile: a skill-category label, a
best-fit career role, and a ranked list of the largest gaps to close.

## What This Project Is Not

- It is **not** a real employability or hiring-outcome predictor. The skill-category model is
  trained on labels derived from unsupervised clustering of synthetic data, not from real
  hiring, job-placement, or performance outcomes. See
  [ML_METHODOLOGY.md](ML_METHODOLOGY.md) for the full explanation of why this distinction
  matters and must be preserved in any user-facing or future documentation text.
- It is **not** based on official industry hiring standards. The 5 career role requirement
  profiles in `backend/app/career_roles.py` are fixed, illustrative numbers chosen for this
  project, not sourced from any hiring dataset or industry body.
- It does **not** currently use real student data. The dataset is synthetic (see
  [DATA_MODEL.md](DATA_MODEL.md) and `ml/data/raw/DATASET_NOTES.md`), generated by
  `ml/generate_synthetic_data.py`, and is designed to be replaceable by real survey data later
  without changing column names, ranges, or downstream code.

## Intended Users

Not documented / requires confirmation — no target-user research, personas, or business
requirements document exists in the repository. The application's own copy addresses

## Relationship to README.md

`README.md` is the project's public-facing overview (audience, features, getting-started
commands). This `docs/` folder is the detailed, authoritative reference — when the two ever
disagree on a specific fact (a command, a metric, a supported role), treat `docs/` as the
source of truth and update `README.md` to match, since it is kept intentionally concise.
